The Official BSP 3-Step Verification Method

Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas

The BSP advises the public to practice the FEEL • LOOK • TILT technique to easily distinguish genuine banknotes from counterfeits without requiring special equipment.

1. FEEL (Substrate & Embossing)
  • Security Paper: 80% cotton, 20% Philippine abaca; distinctly rough and fibrous (never smooth/slippery like ordinary paper).
  • Intaglio Embossed Print: Tactile roughness on the portrait, denomination numerals, and "REPUBLIKA NG PILIPINAS".
  • Tactile Lines: Pairs of embossed short lines on the left and right edges for the visually impaired (1 to 6 pairs; 5 dots on polymer).
2. LOOK (Watermarks & Inks)
  • Shadow Watermark: Multi-tonal portrait and denomination numeral visible when held up against the light.
  • Security Fibers: Red and blue fibers randomly scattered across the paper substrate.
  • Asymmetric Serial Number: Digits ascending in font size from left to right.
  • See-Through Baybayin: Incomplete pre-Hispanic script completes into "PILIPINO" when viewed against the light.
3. TILT (Optical Elements)
  • Security Thread: Metallic windowed or embedded thread bearing "BSP" and denomination amount.
  • Color-Shifting Ink (OVI): Denomination 1000 shifts color from green to blue when tilted.
  • Optically Variable Device (OVD): Reflective metallic patch featuring parrot (₱500) or pearl (₱1,000).
  • Polymer ₱1,000 (2022): Transparent clear window with Philippine eagle and Sampaguita hologram.

Official BSP Protocol: What To Do If You Suspect a Fake Note

BSP Circular & Advisory
  1. Do NOT pass or spend the banknote. Under Philippine law (Article 168 of the Revised Penal Code), knowingly circulating or passing counterfeit currency is punishable by imprisonment.
  2. Hold the banknote carefully and preserve evidence. Avoid excessive handling. Place the note in a clean envelope, transparent plastic sleeve, or document folder to preserve latent fingerprints and forensic marks.
  3. Record receiver details. Note the physical appearance of the person who gave you the note, companions, date, time, and vehicle license plate number if available.
  4. Surrender the note immediately to authorities:
    • Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP): Currency Issue and Integrity Office (CIIO) / Payments and Currency Investigation Group (PCIG), Security Plant Complex, Quezon City, or any BSP Regional Office / Branch.
    • Law Enforcement: Nearest Philippine National Police (PNP) station or National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) Anti-Fraud Division.
    • Authorized Banks: Any commercial bank. Banks are legally mandated by BSP Circulars to issue an official receipt (BSP Confiscation Receipt) and transmit suspect notes to the BSP for technical analysis.
